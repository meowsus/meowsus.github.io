Ok.

I have been partially sober for a month. I qualify my sobriety with the word _partially_ because I am experimenting against my previous abstinent rigidity. Historically, when I choose to abstain from something, it's been _all or nothin'_; a method which explodes in my face as soon as I give into that dirty little addict's voice in my head which tells me "aw, buddy, you can have _just one_..."

When I find where that fucker is hiding, I'll give him the thrashing of his life. Until then he seems quite content to live rent-free in my head.

My decision to stop casually drinking 3-6 alcoholic beverages per night (every night, for the majority of the last two decades) came writhing to life from the union of two events. Event the first was a very strange, abrupt, and tidy ending to a relationship with a woman whom I cared for deeply. Event the second was the signing of a job offer and one that requires me to start work every day at 6AM.

You heard me right: 16 months after losing my job to Skynet I've been selected by a Dutchman to help radically disrupt the cybersecurity industry. I'm a full-time salaried employee again. I'm making adult-money again. I'm no longer living hand-to-mouth. My savings account is receiving small splashes of budgeting overages twice a month, at regular intervals. Ah, financial stability, how I have missed you.

Anyway, in my experience, heartache is more quickly remedied when you can raw-dog your emotions. I can't experience grief when I am actively choosing to give my brain a booze-bath each and every night. I also can't wake up and work when I'm casually hungover each morning. This is why I'm avoiding drinking. It hasn't been difficult.

What has been unexpected is that when I do allow myself an occasional drink I can very quickly go from one to three. If that happens, the next day is difficult because I am at least slightly hungover since drinking and binge-eating late at night go hand in hand for me. Since I've stopped drinking every night, I've started eating less. Therefore a three-drink night will fuck me up hard and fast, like it would to any normal person. That hangover will linger all day and that little voice will tell me that I should probably have a beer that night so I can shake the hangover. I will, and the cycle repeats. It's how I got into this mess in the first place. The trick has been to not just listen to that layabout, but to actually hear what he's saying. He's saying "just stay drunk, bud" and I'm saying "but, man, look at all the shit I can get done if I don't drink."

So we arrive at the point of the post: productivity. My god, the productivity. At work, I've been a thunderous machine this week. I have ramped up in the codebase much more quickly than I had expected to and am taking on two extremely desirable projects at once. One is client-facing and one is a developer-experience task. This is where I love to be, spending 80% of my time on building the house and 20% of my time cleaning up after myself and my roommates. I just finished my third week and my velocity is really starting to skyrocket. I am using my brain again, after 16 months of serious atrophy, and it feels absolutely glorious.

The funny thing is that there's another voice in my head, though. One that has been pushing me to work later into the night than I typically would. That voice sounds oddly similar to that other guy's...

Anyway, I'm not only productive at work, I'm also knocking shit off my TODO lists at home, getting way more focused in on making music again, and working on a bunch of personal projects all in tandem. I re-launched [https://fauxfetus.net] again, this time in Sveltekit, and just this morning had a breakthrough idea that I don't want to talk about because I think it could actually make me some side cash to squirrel away. It's a really good idea. It's one of those ideas you _just know_ is a great one. Simple, impactful, giant audience, stable, and mostly self-sufficient.

I'll let you know more details soon. Hell, I'd even work on it with you if you wanted. I recently collaborated with my friend Joe on the Cookbook app and it was really fun to project manage while working.
